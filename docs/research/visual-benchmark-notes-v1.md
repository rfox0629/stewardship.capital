# Visual Benchmark Research Notes v1

Prepared for USA-185 (Stewardship.Capital visual homepage) and USA-184 (Stewardship Events operating system).

Purpose: establish a quality bar before designing. These are benchmarks, not templates. Nothing below was copied. Each entry records the single principle carried into the Stewardship Capital visual language.

## References and the principle taken from each

### 1. Linear (linear.app)
Observed: full width modular sections, roughly 30 percent copy to 70 percent visual, real product surfaces used as the hero image rather than decorative art, generous margins, accent color reserved for interactive elements only.

Principle taken: **let the artifact do the explaining.** Show the system, do not describe it. Reserve saturated color for things a person can act on.

### 2. Anthropic (anthropic.com)
Observed: warm neutral ground, high contrast type, very generous whitespace, language itself used as the primary visual anchor instead of illustration, tone that reads as considered rather than promotional.

Principle taken: **one sentence can be the image.** A short, well set line of type at large scale carries more credibility than a stock photograph, and it keeps a faith grounded brand from drifting into sentimentality.

### 3. Stripe (stripe.com)
Observed: layered gradient and wave grounds that create depth without competing with content, complex infrastructure decomposed into bento cards, one to three sentences per section maximum.

Principle taken: **depth through layered ground, not through ornament.** Background gradients should recede and give the page atmosphere. Complexity gets decomposed into a small number of legible tiles.

### 4. Vercel (vercel.com)
Observed: strict monochrome discipline, light and dark variants of the same surface shown together, headline plus image plus at most four bullets per section, static composition doing the work instead of animation.

Principle taken: **restraint is a credibility signal.** A near monochrome page with one accent reads as more serious than a multi color page. Motion is optional, composition is not.

### 5. Mercury (mercury.com)
Observed: abstract geometric illustration in place of photography, overlapping circles for a unified money view, roughly 30 words in the hero and 15 to 25 words per feature, trust signals stated plainly.

Principle taken: **diagram the idea rather than photograph the customer.** Abstract geometry communicates a financial system honestly and avoids the stock imagery problem, which matters here because Stewardship Capital has no customer photography yet.

### 6. Greylock and the restrained venture category
Observed: editorial restraint, selective content, typography and spacing carrying the sense of stability and selectiveness rather than visual effects.

Principle taken: **what you leave out is the positioning.** Fewer sections, fewer claims, more space. A stewardship brand should feel unhurried.

### 7. Lux Capital and a16z editorial approach
Observed: ideas and perspective foregrounded, the site reads like a publication with a point of view rather than a product brochure.

Principle taken: **lead with a worldview, not a feature list.** Stewardship as purposeful management of what God has entrusted is the idea. Products are expressions of it and belong lower on the page.

### 8. Awwwards scroll craft category (Locomotive, By-Kin and similar 2025 to 2026 winners)
Observed: the winning work is described as choreography rather than animation. Transitions that carry meaning, scroll sequences that pace a story, micro interactions that reward attention. The best animated sites are also the fastest.

Principle taken: **motion must mean something.** Every animation on this page either draws a connection, reveals a sequence, or marks progress. Nothing moves for decoration, and everything collapses cleanly under prefers-reduced-motion.

## What we deliberately rejected

- WebGL and three dimensional scenes. High cost, fragile on mobile, and they push the brand toward sci fi, which the brief rules out.
- Crypto and dark neon aesthetics. Wrong worldview signal for stewardship.
- Stock photography of smiling families. Reads as insurance marketing and undercuts premium positioning.
- Heavy parallax. Ages badly and fights readability.
- Long explanatory paragraphs. The brief asks for the visual to do the explaining.
